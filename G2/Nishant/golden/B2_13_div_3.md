# B2 Hand Trace — Restoring Division of 13 by 3

We trace unsigned `13 / 3` (`DIVU`) through the restoring-division algorithm used by `G2/Nishant/rtl/divider.v`. The expected quotient is 4 and the expected remainder is 1.

## Initial values

```text
dividend a = 13 = 0b1101
 divisor b =  3 = 0b0011
quotient register starts with |a| = 13
partial remainder starts at 0
```

The hardware always performs 32 iterations for an ordinary divide. The 28 leading zero bits of the 32-bit dividend leave the partial remainder at zero and append zero quotient bits. The meaningful trace below shows the final four input bits `1, 1, 0, 1`.

## Four meaningful restoring steps

For each step, form `shifted = 2 × old_remainder + next_dividend_bit`, then try `trial = shifted − divisor`. If `trial` is nonnegative, retain it and append quotient bit 1; otherwise restore `shifted` and append quotient bit 0.

| Step | Next bit | Old remainder | Shifted remainder | Trial `shifted − 3` | Decision | New remainder | Quotient bit |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | -2 | Negative: restore | 1 | 0 |
| 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 0 | Fits: keep trial | 0 | 1 |
| 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -3 | Negative: restore | 0 | 0 |
| 4 | 1 | 0 | 1 | -2 | Negative: restore | 1 | 0 |

The four quotient bits produced at the end are `0100₂ = 4`. The final partial remainder is `1`.

## Result

```text
13 = 3 × 4 + 1
quotient  = 0x00000004
remainder = 0x00000001
```

Therefore:

- `DIVU(13, 3)` → `0x00000004`
- `REMU(13, 3)` → `0x00000001`
- Since both operands are positive, `DIV(13, 3)` and `REM(13, 3)` give the same bit patterns.

## Connection to the RTL

- `quot` is initialized to `a_abs`; its most-significant remaining bit is read as `quot[31]`.
- `shifted = {rem[31:0], quot[31]}` brings down the next dividend bit.
- `trial = shifted - {1'b0, dvsr}` computes the candidate subtraction.
- `fits = ~trial[32]` selects whether to keep `trial` or restore `shifted`.
- `quot <= {quot[30:0], fits}` shifts the next quotient bit into the low end.

The full 32-step hardware trace includes the leading zero iterations; they do not change the mathematical result.
